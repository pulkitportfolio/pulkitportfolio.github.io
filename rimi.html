The system at a glance

Three products. One operating system for an insurance business.

One place to quote and issue policies. One place to build the training that agents have to pass. One place where those agents learn, get tested and get certified. Each product stands alone, and all three share a single design language.

01 · About

What is RIMI?

RIMI sells travel insurance to people coming to Canada. Four products cover four different situations: Canuck Voyage Travel Medical, Canuck Voyage Non-Medical Travel, Secure Travel for visitors, and Secure Study for international students. Almost none of it is sold by RIMI directly. It is sold through a network of agents.

That network is the whole business, and it creates two problems at once. The agents need a system to quote, issue and track policies. And RIMI needs those agents to actually understand what they are selling, because a travel medical policy mis-sold to a student is a claim that gets denied at a hospital counter.

So I designed both halves: a CRM where a policy goes from a blank form to a paid, documented, searchable record, and a training platform where RIMI builds courses, tests comprehension inside the video itself, and issues the certificate that says an agent is ready to sell.

02 · Problem

Two problems that looked unrelated

On paper this was two briefs. A CRM brief and a training brief. Spending time with how the business actually runs made it clear they were the same brief seen from two ends: RIMI's revenue depends on agents, and nothing was holding either side of that relationship together.

Selling

A quote was a dead end

Pricing happened in one place, the application in another, the policy document somewhere else. Every handover was a chance to re-key the same date of birth and get it wrong.

Selling

Groups broke the flow

Student insurance is not sold one at a time. A college sends sixty names. Sixty identical applications, typed by hand, is not a workflow, it is a data entry job.

Selling

Nothing was findable

A caller says "I bought something in March, I think my wife's name is on it." Without a way to search on partial, fuzzy information, that call has no good ending.

Training

Watching is not learning

Training videos were sent out and marked complete when the runtime ended. Nobody could tell the difference between an agent who studied and a tab left playing in the background.

Training

Certification meant nothing

If completion is measured in minutes elapsed, a certificate certifies attendance. RIMI needed it to certify comprehension.

Both

No view of the network

RIMI could not answer basic questions: which agent tier is producing, who has been trained, who is selling a product they never passed a test on.

“

An agent who does not understand the product is not a training problem. It is a claims problem, six months later. This reframing is why the CRM and the training platform were designed as one system rather than two projects.

03 · The ask

What the project demanded, and what I did about it

Insurance software has a reputation for being ugly and unusable, and it is usually earned honestly: the domain is genuinely complicated. The work was to absorb that complexity into the structure instead of pushing it onto the person filling the form.

The project demanded

A quote that becomes a policy without being retyped

Quoting and applying were separate acts with separate forms, holding most of the same information.

What I did

Made them one three-step flow. Step one prices it, step two completes it, step three pays for it. The quote summary carries forward into the application as read-only fact, so nothing gets asked twice.

The project demanded

One product sold in cohorts, three sold one by one

Secure Study goes to colleges, sixty students at a time. The other three are individual sales.

What I did

Gave only that product a second button. Bulk Upload sits beside Application Form on the catalogue, so the shortcut appears exactly where the need exists and nowhere else.

The project demanded

Finding a record from half-remembered details

Callers rarely have a policy number. They have a first name, a rough date and a product.

What I did

Designed records as search-first, not table-first. Up to fourteen optional criteria with one instruction: fill in as many as you can. Every field narrows, none is required.

The project demanded

Proof that an agent understood the training

Video completion is trivially gamed, and RIMI was certifying on exactly that.

What I did

Moved the test inside the video. Admins place a test at a timestamp, and the video will not advance past it until the learner scores 16 out of 20. Comprehension becomes the condition of progress.

The project demanded

Nine CRM modules and four admin modules, learnable in a day

New agents join constantly. Training people on the tool competes with training them on insurance.

What I did

Built every module from the same three parts: a breadcrumb, one grey criteria card, one primary action underneath. Learn Policies and you have learnt Quotes, Users and Reporting.

05 · Insurance CRM

Where a policy is born

The catalogue is not a marketing page, it is the front door to selling. Four products, and next to each one the action that starts a sale. No browsing, no product detail pages nobody reads. You are here to issue insurance, so the first thing on screen is the button that issues insurance.

The important design decision on this screen is the one that looks like an inconsistency. Only Secure Study carries a second button.

Route one

One person, one form

The three-step application. Used for a couple travelling to Canada, a visitor, a family. Prices live as the form is filled, and additional applicants are added inside the same quote rather than started as new ones.

Route two

Sixty people, one file

Bulk upload for student cohorts. A college drops in a spreadsheet, chooses whether every student gets their own confirmation email or the institution gets one, and submits once.

Quote to policy in three steps

The core flow
1

Get quote

Applicant details and coverage information. Province of residence, policy type, effective and expiry dates, destination, deductible, and whether the trip passes through the US. The price updates at the bottom of the screen as it is filled.

2

Complete application

The quote is restated at the top as settled fact, then contact, residence and payment details are collected. Nothing already answered is asked again.

3

Confirmation

A final review and Pay Now. Previous stays available at every step, so a wrong deductible costs a click, not a restart.

06 · Records

Search first, table second

Most insurance back offices open onto a table of ten thousand rows and dare you to filter it. I inverted that. Policies, Quotes and Users all open onto a single criteria card carrying one line of instruction: fill in as many of the following criteria as you can. No field is mandatory. Every field narrows.

It works because it matches how the information actually arrives. A caller has a first name and a rough month, not a policy number. A manager wants everything one agent sold between two dates. Both are the same screen, filled differently.

The three modules are deliberately near-identical, down to field order and the position of the Search button. An agent who has used one has used all three, which is the point when the network is constantly onboarding new people.

The agent network

Users and permissions

RIMI's agents are not one group. The dashboard tracks three tiers, Mega, Agents and Random, and they behave differently enough that the business plans around the split. So user records carry agent code, company and user type, and creating a user carries an honest warning: changing the user type resets permissions to that type's defaults.

07 · Training admin

Building the training, not just hosting it

The second product is where RIMI's training team works. It is deliberately small: four items in the menu, because a training manager has four jobs. See how the programme is doing, build courses, manage the people taking them, and issue the certificate at the end.

A course here is not a video with a quiz bolted on the back. It is a video, its study material, and a set of tests that each know where they belong inside the runtime. That last part is what makes the whole system work, and it is designed in the next section.

08 · Checkpoints

The idea the whole platform is built around

Every video course has the same weakness. Completion is measured in time elapsed, and time elapses whether or not anyone is watching. RIMI was issuing certificates on that basis, to people who would go out and sell medical insurance to travellers.

So the test stops being something at the end. When a training manager writes a test, they give it a start time in minutes. At that moment the video stops and the test takes over the player. Score 16 out of 20 and the video continues. Score less and it rewinds. You do not fail the course, you rewatch the ten minutes you did not absorb and try the checkpoint again.

The certificate now means an agent answered correctly at three points inside the material, not that a video finished playing. One field in the admin panel, Test Start Time, is what turns a video library into a qualification.

09 · Learner portal

The agent's side of the deal

The learner portal has two menu items. Home and Certificates. That is the entire information architecture, and it is the right amount, because a learner has exactly one job and one reward.

The player screen carries everything needed in one column: the video, its description, and the study materials as a downloadable list underneath. No tabs, no accordions, no hunting for the PDF that was mentioned at minute twelve.

10 · Design system

One system, three products

All three products are built from the same component library: form fields with labels, radio and checkbox groups, date and time pickers, data filters, profile and language menus, tables, pagination and buttons, each as a component set with variants. That is what lets a CRM, an admin panel and a learner portal read as one company rather than three vendors.

Three rules did most of the work. Grey cards hold input, white surfaces hold results. One indigo means action, and nothing else is allowed to be indigo. Every destructive act confirms, every long form states its limits before you hit them.

11 · Learnings

What this project proves about how I work

I look for the real problem behind the brief. This arrived as two requests, a CRM and a training portal. Understanding that RIMI's risk sits with the agent, not the software, is what turned it into one system with a shared purpose.

I design the exception on purpose. One product has a second button. One form asks whether you travel through the US. These are not inconsistencies, they are the places where the domain refuses to be uniform, and pretending otherwise is what makes enterprise tools painful.

I make the boring screens well. Search criteria, password resets, delete confirmations and date pickers are where operational software is actually lived in. They got the same attention as the dashboard.

I design for the person who has to run it. Publish toggles on individual tests, editable policy documents, exportable reports. Wherever the team would otherwise need a developer, I gave them a control instead.
