UX/UI case study · Website redesign

What’s your flavour, Mampster?

A Toronto street food brand wanted every product front and centre. I redesigned their whole website around the shelf, and put their bestselling gol gappa pani flavours right on the hero, one click away.

01 · The brand

Street food with street cred, since 1994

Mampster makes authentic, all-natural Indian street food out of Toronto. The brand started as a no-frills stand in Toronto’s Little India and grew into a packaged food company that has served over a million plates, with products stocked in 200+ local stores and food outlets.

The range is bigger than most people expect. Ready-to-drink gol gappa pani in six flavours is the bestseller, next to tamarind chutneys, fresh green chutney, gol gappa and pani puri packs, chat papri and a canned drinks line. Everything is vegetarian, nut-free and non-GMO, and it ships to customers across Canada, the UK, Australia, New Zealand, Germany and the Netherlands.

02 · The brief

Put the products on stage, not behind the curtain

The client came with a clear ask. The website existed to showcase the products, but the UI was not doing them justice. My job was to fix the UI and redesign the whole website so that every product is presented properly, in the brand’s logo and colours, with the bestseller leading from the front.

Goal 01

Present the full range

Drinks, chutneys and snacks all needed a proper home on the site, so a visitor can browse the whole shelf without digging.

Goal 02

Lead with the bestseller

Gol gappa pani flavours are what Mampster is known for. They had to be the first thing every visitor sees, not one card among many.

Goal 03

Carry the brand everywhere

The logo and the signature Mampster pink had to run through every screen, so the site feels like the packaging on the shelf.

“

If the products are the story, the website should read like a shelf. Every design decision that followed came from this one line. The site is organised product-first, and the hero is the shelf’s eye-level row.

03 · The hero idea

“What’s your flavour?” Click a bottle and find out

The hero opens with the question the brand asks its customers, and a row of six bottle silhouettes, one per pani flavour, each in its own colour. Click any bottle and the real product packshot swaps in beside it, label and all, from Spiced Tamarind to Spiced Blueberry. The bestseller is not just shown, it is playable.

05 · Colour system

One pink to rule the page, six colours to sell the range

Mampster’s packaging pink is loud, and that is the point. Instead of taming it, the redesign gives it a system. The signature pink owns the brand moments, soft pink and cream keep long stretches easy on the eyes, and each flavour keeps its own packaging colour so the range reads at a glance, on the hero, in product cards and in the drinks carousel.

07 · The menu

Navigation that stays out of the food’s way

The header keeps just two actions, Contact Us and Menu. The menu opens as a clean full-screen overlay with three destinations, and the item you are pointing at fills in with the full brand pink while the others wait in a lighter tint. Simple to build, impossible to get lost in.

08 · Outcome

What the redesign handed the client

The brief asked for a website that presents every product in the brand’s own colours. The redesign delivers that, and a few things the client did not know to ask for.

✓

The whole range on display

Drinks, chutneys and snacks each have a proper shelf, so no product hides behind another.

★

The bestseller leads

Gol gappa pani owns the first screen of the site, exactly where the brand’s best story should be.

✎

A brand-true UI

The packaging pink, the logo and the flavour colours now run through every page, so site and shelf finally match.

▶

A working prototype

Every interaction, from bottle swaps to menu states, is prototyped in Figma, so development starts with answers instead of questions.

09 · Learnings

What this project proves about how I work

I let the product carry the design. Mampster’s packshots and colours were the strongest asset on the table, so the UI frames them instead of competing with them.

I design interactions people want to repeat. The bottle swap costs one click and rewards it instantly. That is what makes a visitor try all six flavours instead of reading about one.

I treat brand colour as a system, not decoration. One loud pink could have eaten the page. Splitting it into brand, canvas and flavour roles kept it loud where it sells and quiet where it reads.

I ship the full picture. Pages, menu states and a clickable prototype, so the client can feel the site working before a single line of code exists.
